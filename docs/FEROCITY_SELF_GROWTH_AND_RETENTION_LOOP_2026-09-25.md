# Ferocity Self-Growth and Retention Loop

Status: implemented locally; migration 230 and the application changes await the next authorized deployment.

## Business objective

Ferocity is its own first customer. The operating goal is not content volume, clicks, or a pile of marketing tools. It is profitable customer growth:

`campaign -> relevant promise -> useful diagnosis -> consented lead -> follow-up -> signup/payment -> first value -> retained usage -> expansion/referral -> measured revenue and margin -> learning`

## What now runs without repeated owner prompting

1. Ask Ferocity captures the prospect's goal, diagnostic context, source, and consent.
2. Ferocity enrolls the prospect in a short self-growth sequence after the live chat goes quiet.
3. The sequence uses the actual diagnosis, links to the truthful pricing/start path, stops on reply, and ends after three touches.
4. Consent, suppression, provider health, capability trust, workspace authority, delivery evidence, and rate/cost controls still govern execution.
5. The AI Growth Department now runs from the recurring business-automation loop once per day rather than requiring someone to press the Growth button.
6. It combines the shared marketing brain, composable growth intelligence, closed-loop revenue controller, opportunity execution/preparation, and organic-content learning.
7. A customer activation and retention loop now measures first value, onboarding progress, connected context, real operating records, recency, and billing state.
8. At-risk workspaces receive one concrete next-best action inside Ferocity Chat instead of a settings dump. Daily lifecycle snapshots make cohorts, time-to-value, retention, and churn-risk measurable.

## Persuasion rules

Ferocity should be persuasive because it is relevant, useful, credible, and easy—not because it traps people.

- Match the campaign promise to the visitor's actual goal.
- Give useful value before asking for commitment.
- Ask only for information that changes the recommendation.
- Reduce choices to the best next action while keeping alternatives available.
- Preserve conversation context so people do not repeat themselves.
- Use concrete proof and real business outcomes; never fabricate scarcity, testimonials, activity, or results.
- Make total pricing and outside-provider costs clear before purchase or activation.
- Stop nurture on reply, opt-out, conversion, or suppression.
- Keep cancellation and permission revocation straightforward.
- Prefer fast first value over tours, feature lists, and setup theater.

These rules align with Baymard's large-scale findings on checkout friction and account-creation abandonment, Amplitude's 2025 product data linking early activation to longer retention, Stripe's guidance to measure conversion and retention by stage/cohort, and the FTC's warning against hidden terms and obstructive cancellation.

## Metrics Ferocity should optimize

Primary:

- qualified-lead to paid-customer conversion
- median time from signup to first useful result
- day-7 activation
- 30/60/90-day customer and revenue retention
- gross and net revenue retention
- contribution margin after provider and support costs

Guardrails:

- opt-out, complaint, refund, and dispute rate
- failed/blocked delivery rate
- support burden and unresolved issue age
- provider cost per acquired and retained customer
- owner approvals and interruptions per realized outcome
- false claims, policy violations, and trust incidents (target: zero)
